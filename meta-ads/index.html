The short answer

  1. Meta is now a real channel for AI sales agents, and it is crowded at the top of funnel. Pancake (270 live ads), Alta (120), Mailscale (118), Gojiberry (109), Explee (81), Origami (58) and PumpGTM (26) are all spending. Clay's own ad says it spent $50,000 on Meta in a quarter for $1.27M in pipeline ("budget holders do doomscroll Instagram"). Valley has never run a Meta ad, 11x stopped all 104 of its Meta ads (median run: 1 day) and runs on LinkedIn instead.
  2. "Paste your website" is the category's shared hook, not a differentiator. Explee (all 77 live creatives), Origami, Gojiberry, Mailscale and Pancake all lead with it, and Valley's and our own landing pages do too. Closefast has to win on what comes after the URL box.
  3. The one angle that dominates 2026 is riding the AI assistant wave: "I asked Claude...", "Claude now finds your buyers", "Turn Claude into your sales assistant", "Run outbound from your AI chats". It is in 15 brands' live ads. Gojiberry's "Claude told me" selfie videos are among the longest-running agent ads in the set (111 to 135 days).
  4. The ads that last are people talking to a phone camera, plus a few native-looking statics. Long runners are UGC selfie videos (Gojiberry), "VP of sales" confession videos and a named AI employee (Alta's "Katie", 18 ads on one video for 153 days), partner testimonials (Smartlead, 387 days), and statics that look like a tweet, a whiteboard, a billboard or a Calendly inbox.
  5. The winning offer is free plus a countable first result: "Get your first 3 to 5 clients free" (Explee), "[Free Trial] 3x more replies" (Gojiberry), "40 contacts, 3 positive replies" (Salesforge). Nobody offers free forever. Nobody sends from their own warm inboxes with nothing to connect. Only AiSDR prices the outcome in its ads ("$1.50 per person contacted"), and no agent attacks seat pricing. Those three are Closefast's openings.
  6. Launch with 12 concepts across 3 campaigns (prospecting tests, scale, retargeting), founder-led video plus native statics, one fixed offer line on every ad, and kill or keep on a 7 to 10 day read like the testers do. Details in the plan.

Who is spending, and how

Two operating modes show up. Testers launch many ads and kill most within days (Explee: 68 of 81 live ads are under 30 days old; Mailscale 96 of 118; Pancake 147 of 270; PumpGTM all of them). Holders found something and keep paying for it (Alta: live ads have a median age of 126 days; Smartlead's oldest live ad is 387 days; Instantly's stopped ads ran a median 90 days). The holders tell you what works. The testers tell you what is being tried right now. Click a column to sort.

12 patterns that keep showing up in what lasts

Each pattern below is backed by ads that are still running after weeks or months, or by the same idea showing up across several brands. Click any ad to open it in the Meta Ad Library.

Pattern explorer

Every tagged creative counted by angle, hook, format, offer, proof and audience. Share weights each brand equally, so Pancake's 270 ads do not drown out Explee's 81. Median age is how long the live creatives using it have been running. Read the two columns together: high share with low age means crowded and churning; low share with high age means a quiet angle that holds.

What the tags say for AI sales agents (live creatives, Bland and Retell excluded because their ads are not about sales):
  • Formats that hold: founder-to-camera video (87% of live ones are 30+ days old), screen demos (90%), big-number statics (median age 70 days and the best scroll-stopping read, 4.1 of 5), comparisons (57%), creator UGC (56%). Formats that churn: person-photo statics (29%), motion graphics (40%, median 5 days), skits (29%), illustrations (39%).
  • Angles: "saves you time / runs on autopilot" and "more meetings" are the most common. Speed to lead holds longest (median 128 days: Alta's "Alex" and Voxr). Price arguments are young (34% past 30 days), which is a test in progress, not a verdict.
  • Hooks: pain callouts and result claims lead on volume (13 and 12 brands). Questions (70%) and curiosity gaps (73%) hold longest. Offer-first hooks churn (5% past 30 days): the free offer belongs in the second line or the headline, not the hook.
  • Offers: a free trial is the default (7 brands). Credit packs (Explee) churn fastest, but Explee keeps the same offer copy and swaps visuals, so that is visual churn, not offer churn.
  • Caveat: the 30+ day share is biased against brands testing right now (Mailscale's whole batch is 14 days old). Use it with the brand table above.

Method and caveats

  • Source: Meta Ad Library via ScrapeCreators (through treg), pulled 31 Oct 2026. Every live ad per page, plus ads that started in 2026 and have since stopped. Each brand's page was verified by matching its ads' landing URLs to the brand's domain (page-name search alone returned wrong pages, e.g. "Jungler" resolved to an Italian camera store and was dropped).
  • Creative: ads that share the same copy and the same first image or video collapse into one creative. "Ads using it" counts how many ad IDs reuse it, a sign of scaling across ad sets.
  • Persistence, not performance: the library has no spend, reach or results for commercial ads. Live age and completed run length are reported separately and never folded into one score, because young ads cannot have long runs yet.
  • Tagging: each creative's still image, copy, on-image text (on-device OCR) and video script were tagged against a fixed taxonomy (angle, hook, format, offer, proof, audience, tone, a 1 to 5 read of scroll-stopping clarity). Brands with hundreds of variants were capped for tagging; shares weight every brand equally.
  • Gaps: 207 videos had no speech to transcribe (music-only), and those were read from copy and thumbnail. Pages that run ads only under a person's name (founder pages) are found only when their ads link to the brand domain.
