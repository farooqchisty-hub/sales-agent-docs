One currency for everything: credits, pay as you go, free credits for everyone

This replaces the free plan of the business thesis (a starter boost plus a monthly allowance, each counted per feature) with a single credit balance. Every feature is open to every account. Features that cost us money to deliver use credits, each at its own rate. Every account gets free credits. It covers all of Closefast, including Signals. v1.1 (7 Nov) follows the Brevo ecommerce pilot: see Signals v4.

In one line: 1 credit = $0.01, always. Every account gets 1,500 free credits at signup (they last 30 days) and 500 free credits every month (no rollover). Paid credits come in packs and never expire. The agent's loop is free: thinking, replies, leads, meetings, the inbox, exports. Credits only pay for fuel: finding people, signals, sending from our inboxes, premium writing, branded inboxes.

Why credits instead of a free plan plus a wallet

One meter, any mix

Today a user tracks 3 free allowances (prospects, prospects worked, emails), each with its own limit and reset date, plus a wallet in dollars. With credits, there's one number. A user who only wants signals spends everything on signals; another spends it all on sending.

Free is the whole product

There are no feature gates, so a free account can try Signals, premium writing and everything else on its free credits. People upgrade because they've seen it work, not because a button is locked.

We can price each feature on its own

When a data provider gets cheaper or an email costs us more, we change one rate. There are no plans to reprice and no grandfathering. Clay, Explee and the model APIs work this way, so buyers already understand it.

The upgrade moment is natural

Credits run out while results are on screen: replies in the inbox, prospects found but locked. That converts better than a plan comparison table.

The rules

  1. 1 credit = $0.01, fixed. Prices are shown in credits with the dollar value next to them. We never quietly change what a credit is worth.
  2. Charge for fuel, never for the engine or the outcome. Reading the website, campaigns, previews, test mode, reply handling, leads, meetings, call briefs, exports and support use no credits. We never charge per reply, lead or meeting (thesis decision).
  3. Charge when value is delivered, never for attempts. A qualified prospect placed in a campaign, an email actually sent. Scanning, posts, drops, duplicates, agencies set aside and people without a deliverable email are free.
  4. Refund what we got wrong. A found email that bounces gets its credits back automatically. So does a send that failed on our side.
  5. Never back-bill and never surprise. Every campaign and signal shows its expected credits before it starts. When credits run out, new work pauses, and nothing piles up to be charged later.
  6. Spend order: welcome credits first (they expire soonest), then this month's free credits, then paid credits (which never expire).

Spend controls

  1. An estimate before anything starts. A campaign shows its expected credits a week at the chosen pace, and a signal watch shows expected prospects and credits a month, worked out from measured volume and fit rate. A Signals backfill (7, 14 or 30 days) shows about how many buyers and credits it would add before you confirm.
  2. A weekly budget per campaign (this replaces Signals' "daily target"). The engine fills it hottest first. Over-budget prospects are kept as found-but-locked, with the name hidden and no charge.
  3. An optional monthly cap for the account. When it's hit, everything new pauses.
  4. A yield guard on every signal watch (v1.1). A watch may spend on data at most 30% of what its delivered buyers earn (40 credits each), measured over the last 7 days. It applies once the watch has spent more than $0.40 and has nothing left in its pipeline. Past that line, the watch pauses itself. It tells the customer how many buyers it found for how much searching, so they can widen or replace it. Resuming restarts its count. Example: a watch that spent $0.60 on data in 7 days needs 5 buyers ($2.00 earned) to keep running.
  5. A free search cap (v1.1). Accounts that have never bought credits can spend at most $0.50 of data a day on Signals. Buying any pack lifts it. Ops can override it per workspace.
  6. Follow-ups are reserved upfront. When a prospect's first email goes out, credits for its remaining follow-ups are held, so a sequence never stalls halfway. When it ends early (a reply, an opt-out, a bounce), the unused hold is released.
  7. At zero credits:
    • No new prospects or first emails go out.
    • Sequences that already started keep running on their held credits.
    • Signals drop to a weekly scan and fill the found-but-locked list.
    • Replies are still read and answered.
    A top-up restarts from that day, with no backlog.
  8. Low-balance email when the balance covers less than 3 days of the current pace.

Upgrade moments (what makes people buy credits)

  • Found but locked: "23 more prospects engaged with your competitors this week." Their title, company and reason are shown; the name and email are hidden.
  • Proof before the deadline: on day 23, "Your welcome credits found 48 prospects, sent 190 emails and got 4 replies and 1 lead."
  • Results in the inbox: when a reply comes in and the balance is low, the top-up link sits next to it.
  • Capacity: when an account keeps hitting 25 free emails a day, it's offered a pack or its own branded inbox.
  • Hosting gives us visibility: we can see every account that keeps running dry, and Closefast itself can email them.
