Signals: find people showing buying intent in the last 24 hours, and email them the next day

The Jungler-style module inside Closefast. v2 replaces the "watch a few competitor pages" idea with keywords, intent and audience, after live tests on real LinkedIn data and research into how Jungler and similar tools work. v3 (6 Nov) moved it onto credits. v4 (7 Nov) records what the Brevo ecommerce pilot changed. It follows on from the business thesis and the V1 build plan.

In one line: a signal isn't a page. It's a conversation from the last 24 hours, found by keywords (brands, category, pain phrases), scored for intent (asking, complaining, comparing, switching, pricing), and kept only when the audience is buyers and the person fits (they run a store). It runs every day. Each buyer delivered costs 40 credits ($0.40, up from 15 in v4), from the same credits as the rest of Closefast, and you set a weekly budget. Searching, posts, rejects and agencies set aside are never billed. Where this page says "buyer", it means a qualified signal prospect; a lead is still an interested reply.

2. Cheaper lookups

  • Person lookups move to BlitzAPI at $0.0005 (was $0.0015). The old provider stays as the fallback.
  • Company lookups go to BlitzAPI first, then the old provider. Crustdata's free identify was tried on the pilot and dropped: at 30 calls a minute and up to 14 seconds an answer, it stalled whole runs.
  • Expected data cost: about $0.07 per buyer.

3. Yield guard on every watch

Each watch may spend on data at most 30% of what its delivered buyers earn (40 credits each), measured over the last 7 days. The guard applies once the watch has spent more than $0.40 and has nothing left in its pipeline. Past that line, the watch pauses itself. It tells the customer how many buyers it found for how much searching, so they can widen or replace it. Resuming restarts its count.

Example: a watch that spent $0.60 on data in 7 days needs 5 buyers ($2.00 earned) to keep running.

4. Free search cap

Accounts that have never bought credits can spend at most $0.50 of data a day on Signals. Ops can override it per workspace.

5. Filters become labels

Stays a filter (it defines a buyer)

  • The intent itself
  • A real role: not engineers, writers, students, recruiters or job seekers
  • Not a competitor's staff
  • Has what the customer sells to (a store, for ecommerce)
  • A verified email
  • Not already contacted

Becomes a label (the customer filters by it)

  • Country
  • Company size
  • Store platform

A customer can still opt in to "only these countries" or a size range.

  • One rule stays on by default: we skip countries where cold business email needs prior consent (Germany), unless the customer turns that off.
  • "Website did not load" now passes instead of dropping.
  • The fit score stops scoring country and size.
  • On the pilot, this would have taken 16 buyers to about 40.

6. Agency owners become partners

Agency founders, owners and CEOs (199 of the 806 agencies in the pilot) are delivered as a separate "Agency partners" segment with a partner pitch. They're charged like any buyer. Freelancers and agency staff stay out. When credits are short, buyers are delivered before partners.

7. Backfill is a choice, the daily window is not

  • The daily run reads the last 30 hours (24 plus 6 of overlap). That's plumbing, not a setting.
  • The backfill is what the customer chooses when a campaign or a new watch starts: 7 days (default), 14 or 30. The maximum is 30.
  • Same price per buyer, so more backfill means more credits at the same margin.
  • Signals older than 7 days are labelled cool.
  • Before confirming, the page shows about how many buyers and credits the backfill would add.

8. A "Who counts as a buyer" panel

One panel on the Signals page edits all of this: the labels and opt-in filters, the consent-country rule, the agency setting and the backfill. It shows a live estimate: "about N buyers a week (now M)". Saving sends people we already checked back through the new rules, at no extra data cost.

9. Prospects from data stay 8 credits for now

Finding prospects from data (not signals) stays 8 credits. The estimated cost is about $0.03 each, so about 60% margin. We measure it on the first real run before repricing.

What we copy

  • Daily runs on a small overlapping window, with a backfill the customer picks when a campaign or watch starts (7, 14 or 30 days; v4)
  • Engager re-reads at 1, 3 and 7 days (Jungler)
  • Keyword groups: brand, competitor, category (Octolens)
  • Rank creators by how much of their audience matches the buyer (Jungler Discovery)
  • Cost estimate before saving, spend cap, hard pause (Clay, Unify)
  • Dismissing a person or company excludes them for months (Amplemarket)

What we beat

  • Fixed intent labels on comments as well as posts; Jungler only says match or no match, and only on posts
  • Automatic engager pulls on keyword posts that pass intent; Jungler makes this manual
  • Creators found and tracked automatically from the daily keyword run; others make you name 3 to 5
  • Bill only buyers delivered; Jungler and Octolens charge for rows they then filter out
  • No backlog drain after a pause, and a warning when a broad keyword would hit the daily cap
  • The store and email-tool check, and the email actually written and sent; none of them do both

Who qualifies: the definition we bill on

We charge 40 credits ($0.40, up from 15 in v4, see the Credits plan) only when all five pass and the person has been placed in a campaign. Agency owners delivered as Agency partners are charged the same. Each gate stores its result and reason, so any prospect can be traced back to exactly why they qualified.

A small model reads the text together with the person's headline, in batches of 20, and returns the intent, the heat and a one-line reason. 3. Who fits

  • Free filters first, on the headline: the watched competitor's staff, our own staff, engineers, writers, recruiters, students, job seekers and people open to work are dropped.
  • Paid checks for survivors: profile, then current company, then website, then a homepage fetch. That gives the store platform (Shopify, Woo, BigCommerce, Magento), the email tool in use, and staff and industry. B2B accounts use staff and industry instead of the store check.
  • Exclusions: stores already running Brevo or Sendinblue, existing customers from an uploaded domain list, anyone another of your campaigns or the 60-day pool cooldown already holds, and (v4, on by default) people in countries where cold business email needs prior consent, such as Germany.
  • Labels, not filters (v4): country, company size and store platform are shown on each buyer, and the customer filters by them. "Website did not load" passes.
  • Agency owners as partners (v4): agency founders, owners and CEOs go to an "Agency partners" segment with a partner pitch, charged like any buyer. Freelancers and agency staff stay out. Each campaign picks: skip agencies, agency owners as partners (default), or agencies are the main buyer.

Timing

  • Once a day, early morning in the account's timezone, we read posts from the last 30 hours. The 6-hour overlap closes gaps, and posts are de-duplicated by post id.
  • Backfill (v4, replaces the fixed 7-day first run): when a campaign or a new watch starts, the customer picks 7 days (default), 14 or 30. The maximum is 30. The price per buyer is the same, so more backfill means more credits at the same margin. Signals older than 7 days are labelled cool. Before confirming, the page shows about how many buyers and credits it would add. After that, the daily 30-hour window is plumbing, not a setting.
  • Re-reads: a post that turned out on topic with a buyer audience gets its engagers read again 1, 3 and 7 days after it was posted (Jungler's schedule), then never again. Most engagement arrives in the first 2 days.
  • Email timing: the first email goes out at least 24 hours after the engagement. A hot comment found this morning gets its email tomorrow morning. Polling every hour would add cost and gain nothing, because of this rule.
  • Free accounts run weekly, with the same 7-day window. From v4, accounts that have never bought credits also spend at most $0.50 of data a day on Signals.

How much each customer gets (credits, pay as you go)

Signals runs on the same credits as the rest of Closefast (see the Credits plan). There's no separate Signals plan. The one Signals-only limit is the v4 free search cap below.

  1. A buyer delivered costs 40 credits ($0.40, up from 15 in v4). It's charged when the prospect is placed in a campaign. Scanning, posts, drops, duplicates, near misses, agencies set aside and people without a deliverable email cost nothing.
  2. Yield guard per watch (v4). A watch may spend on data at most 30% of what its delivered buyers earn, over the last 7 days, once it has spent more than $0.40 and has nothing left in its pipeline. Past that, it pauses itself and says how many buyers it found for how much searching. Resuming restarts its count.
  3. Free search cap (v4). Accounts that have never bought credits spend at most $0.50 of data a day on Signals. Ops can override it per workspace.
  4. Free credits work here like everywhere else. Every account's 1,500 welcome credits and 500 monthly credits can go on Signals, data prospects or sending, in any mix.
  5. Each campaign has a weekly credit budget. The engine fills it hottest first. Anything beyond the budget is kept as "found, over budget", showing title, company and reason with the name hidden, so topping up has a visible reason.
  6. At zero credits: watches keep scanning weekly and fill the found-but-locked list, so the reason to top up stays fresh.
  7. Caps pause, they never back-bill. After a top-up, watches resume from that day; there's no backlog drain like Jungler's renewal.
  8. Sending capacity is parked (Farooq, 6 Nov) and gets decided later.
  9. Broad keywords get a warning. When a keyword would return more posts a day than its run can read, the editor says so and suggests narrowing it with an AND, so no matches are lost silently.

Setup and screens

  • Onboarding proposes everything. It already reads the website and works out the buyers. Now it also proposes brands (competitors it found), category keywords, pain phrases and 5 seed creators, all with one tick. The backfill (7 days by default) shows real buyers within minutes.
  • Signals page:
    • A funnel: posts read, on topic, engagers, fit, buyers, contacted, replies.
    • Each watch with its yield.
    • Tracked creators with their audience fit %.
    • A live feed of people with their heat, intent, a quote of what they said, the reason, and labels (country, size, platform) to filter by.
    • A "Who counts as a buyer" panel (v4): labels and opt-in filters, the consent-country rule, the agency setting and the backfill, with a live "about N buyers a week (now M)" estimate. Saving sends already-checked people back through the new rules at no extra data cost.
    • A paused watch shows its yield guard result: buyers found for how much searching, with widen or replace.
  • "Wrong match" on any person teaches the filter (the same learned-rules store as Teach in the Inbox), and "not this company" excludes the company for 6 months.
  • Cost estimate before saving a watch: expected buyers a month and the cost, from the keyword's daily volume and the measured fit rate. The backfill picker shows about how many buyers and credits it would add.
  • Copy rule, enforced in code: the signal picks the angle only. The email never mentions the post, the comment, the like or a competitor's name. That's a hard block that no approval can override.

Each stage runs inside the existing 5-minute scheduler pass with a time budget and resumes on the next pass, so it never starves sending.   • Competitor pages and executives, plus posts tagging them

  • Merchants: run a store, in a founder, marketing, ecommerce or retention role. v4: store platform, staff count and country are labels, not gates (v3 had 2 to 200 staff and six markets). Germany is skipped by default.

  • Excluded: stores on Brevo, competitor staff, anyone the creator engine or Partner Voices has contacted

  • Merchants campaign: live in test mode (previews come to you)

  • Weekly budget about 2,300 credits, hottest first. At v4 prices a fully worked signal prospect is 58 credits (40 + 3 + 15), so that covers about 40 a week, not the 70 planned in v3.
