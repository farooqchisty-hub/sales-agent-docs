Closefast end to end: who uses it, what happens, and where it runs

One page with the whole product as diagrams: the big picture, the customer's journey, where prospects come from, the 5-minute agent pass, an email's life, the money, the operator, and the code. Each diagram has a short walkthrough under it.

In one line: a business owner signs up and pastes their website. The agent works out who buys from them. The owner gets prospects from any source into a Pipeline and chooses who to start. A pass that runs every 5 minutes then writes, checks, sends and follows up emails, reads the replies, answers them and books meetings. Every step is paid for in credits, and owners who bring their own list and inbox pay nothing.

1. The big picture

Three kinds of people touch the system. Everything runs on Cloudflare: one Worker serves both the dashboard and the API, with one database. Outside services supply data, AI, email and payments.

  • Front door: everyone reaches the app at closefast-cloud.pages.dev. Dashboard files load straight away, and anything under /api, /auth or /oauth goes to the Worker's code.
  • One Worker holds the dashboard, the API, the agent, billing and the admin routes. One D1 database stores everything.
  • The cron wakes the agent every 5 minutes, but only acts when the scheduler switch is on. It's off until you say go.

3. Where prospects come from

Four sources, one shared intake. Each source runs the same checks before it charges, and charges only for people with a checked work email.

  • Search people is the Explee-style path: describe the buyers, see the count and a sample, then buy as many as you want. Saved searches continue from where they stopped, so nobody is bought twice.
  • Signals watch LinkedIn for people who engaged with posts about the topic or a competitor. They go through the same checks plus an AI fit score, and a yield guard pauses watches that cost more than they deliver.
  • Your list is free. With your own inbox, the agent's work on those people is free too (up to 1,500 new people a month).
  • Tags (role, country, size, platform, heat) are labels you filter by later, not filters that block delivery.

4. The 5-minute agent pass (the cron)

This is the engine. Every 5 minutes the cron checks the switch. If it's on, it runs one pass for every active workspace, and each step is isolated so one failure doesn't stop the rest.

flowchart TD
  CR["Cron fires every 5 minutes"] --> SW{"Scheduler switch on?"}
  SW -->|"off (today)"| X["Nothing happens"]
  SW -->|"on"| POOL["Read our warm inboxes, once for everyone"]
  POOL --> WS["For each active workspace, in its own sealed context"]
  WS --> S1["1. Read new email from the workspace's inboxes"]
  S1 --> S2["2. Handle replies: classify, act, draft answers"]
  S2 --> S3["3. Spot meetings booked through the booking link"]
  S3 --> S4["4. Web research, only for open requests"]
  S4 --> S5["5. Signals: read LinkedIn, check people, deliver to Pipeline"]
  S5 --> S6["6. Write first emails for people who were started"]
  S6 --> S7["7. Follow-ups and win-backs that are due"]
  S7 --> S8["8. Send what's queued: send window, daily inbox caps,
credits, test mode, do-not-contact"]
  S8 --> S9["9. Daily jobs and the owner's summary email"]
  • Order matters: replies are read before anything is drafted or sent, so a reply cancels the next follow-up.
  • Locks stop two passes from drafting or sending the same email twice.
  • The operator can also run a single step for one workspace without the cron, which is how the pilot was run.

9. Safety built in

  • Nothing sends by accident: the scheduler switch, "I choose" by default, approve the first 20, test mode, send windows and daily caps, and do-not-contact checked everywhere.
  • Tenants stay apart: every query is scoped to one workspace, and a test calls every route as another workspace.
  • Untrusted text stays data: LinkedIn posts, websites, CSVs and replies never act as instructions, and the dashboard escapes everything it shows.
  • Accounts: login lockout, sessions that end on logout and on password change, and confirmed emails before Closefast mails them.
  • Server-side fetches can't reach internal addresses. Security headers are set on every page.
