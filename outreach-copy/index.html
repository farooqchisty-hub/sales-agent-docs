COLD EMAIL FRAMEWORKUpdated 9 Nov 2026live in the writer

The one framework every first email follows

Built from 10 GitHub repos and agent skills, plus vendor data from about 1.3 billion sent emails (Gong, Hunter, Belkins, Instantly, Smartlead, Lavender, lemlist) and the named practitioners. It's in the writer and enforced by the checks, and all 32 Brevo first emails were redrafted with it.

In one line: sound like one person who looked at their business and cares how it goes. Open on something true about them, name one problem the way it feels in everyday words, say who we are only in paragraph 3 along with what changes for them, and ask one real question about them. 70 to 85 words. The subject is a small detail from their world.

1. The shape: notice, problem, who and what changes, ask

Four short paragraphs, most sentences under 18 words. It has to fit a phone screen. Changed 9 Nov after the owner read the queue: the 8 Nov version got to the product too fast, leaned on product terms and sounded straight rather than genuine.

1. NoticeLine 1 is about them and goes one step past the fact: what it means for them, the way a friend in the trade would say it. A small honest reaction is welcome. Never starts with "I" or "We" and never grades them.
2. ProblemOne problem, the way it feels when it happens, in everyday words. Said about people like them, never as a claim about a setup we can't see. No product terms, no stock pivots like "Separate thing".
3. Who and what changesThe first time our name appears. One short clause on who we are, then what changes for them as an outcome they'd feel, naming their product, customers or clients.
4. AskOne short, real question about them, under 12 words, with no meeting or time in it. It's the only question mark in the email.

Taking their side: when their own words show they're in a busy season, the email says we wouldn't change anything right now and asks about afterwards.

Why problem before product: Gong's 28 million emails show pitching cuts replies by up to 57%. Why an interest ask: Gong's CTA study of 304,174 emails found asking about interest beats asking for a meeting on cold emails.

2. The premise ladder: what line 1 stands on

The writer always uses the highest step it has, in Becc Holland's order: content they wrote beats content they engaged with, which beats company facts. One premise per email; anything else waits for a follow-up.

3. Subject lines

  • 2 to 4 words, lowercase, a small specific detail from their world that only makes sense to them: "those foam filters", "running out of scoops", "reporting going dark".
  • A statement, never a question (Lavender: questions cut opens 56%). No first name (Salesloft via Lavender: 12% fewer replies). No numbers, no product name, no "quick question", no fake "re:" or "fwd:", never "your post".
  • Personalised to the topic, not the person: Belkins' 5.5 million emails show personalised subjects at 7% replies against 3% for generic.
  • Follow-ups reply in the same thread, so they keep the first subject.

4. Follow-ups

  • Same person, same voice, a short note in the same thread: 45 to 60 words, one or two short paragraphs, one real question about them.
  • Follow-up 1: a different way the problem shows up for them, with no meeting ask. The middle ones: one small practical idea they can use even if they never reply. The last: about 20 words (under 30 is the limit), makes a no easy and leaves the door open.
  • Never repeats the earlier email and never chases: no "just checking in", "following up" or "bumping this".
  • Follow-ups get the same library, variety check and second-model review as first emails.

5a. How the standard holds without anyone watching

  • Approved examples. Each draft is shown three emails the owner approved, matched to what we know about this person and whether they're a store, an agency or neither. Different people see different examples. Approving or editing a draft adds it to the library. An account with none yet gets built-in examples from invented sellers, so no product claim can leak.
  • Different problem paragraphs. A brief with no problem angles gets six written once from the brief and kept on that version. Each person is given one, and follow-up 1 is given a different one.
  • Better models where it counts. Paying accounts write with Claude Sonnet 5.5 and are reviewed by Claude Haiku 5.5. Never-paid accounts stay on open models (GLM, reviewed by gpt-oss-120b) with their own review bar. An owner who set their own model keeps it.
  • Calibrated bar. Tested on 104 real emails: it passed 44 of the 52 the owner approved and 4 of the 52 AI drafts he wouldn't send.
  • A bad sentence never costs someone their place. Craft failures hold the draft for 6 hours and try again. After three holds it goes to Approvals. Only a draft that can't be made true or safe counts against the lead.
  • The exam. npm run deploy runs the tests, and any change to the writer, the checks, the examples or the model routing must pass scripts/eval-writer.mjs on invented prospects with the real models before it can ship.

GitHub repos and agent skills (read in full)

  • coreyhaines31/marketingskills (cold-email and prospecting skills, 59k stars): the "delete the opener" test, interest asks, a two-step research process, and "no hook found" as a valid result.
  • ethanplusai/sellbird (4.8k stars): one signal per email, stale signals expire, and "a wrong specific is worse than a right generic".
  • growthenginenowoslawski/coldoutboundskills: posts from the last 3 months only, skip personal or charged posts, never invent a post.
  • Cold-IQ/ColdIQ-s-GTM-Skills: what the prospect wrote ranked as the most valuable data; strong vs lite hooks.
  • swan-gtm/gtm-skills (signal-anchored message): open on the change, never hint at how the lead was found.
  • oneshot-agent/oneshot-gtm: a 12 to 22 word opener with one verifiable fact, and no product in line 1.
  • naveedharri/benai-skills: cite a post only if it relates to what you sell; paraphrase, never quote.
  • julienamorgan/signal-prospecting-kit: use the topic, never the platform.
  • BrianRWagner/ai-marketing-claude-code-skills: personalisation tiers by how much is known.
  • tech-leads-club/agent-skills: a 3-line email (pain, proof, ask).
