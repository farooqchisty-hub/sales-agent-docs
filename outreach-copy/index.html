COLD EMAIL FRAMEWORK8 Nov 2026live in the writer, deploy 387064b0

The one framework every first email follows

Built from 10 GitHub repos and agent skills, plus vendor data from about 1.3 billion sent emails (Gong, Hunter, Belkins, Instantly, Smartlead, Lavender, lemlist) and the named practitioners. It's in the writer and enforced by the checks, and all 32 Brevo first emails were redrafted with it.

In one line: open on the strongest true thing we know about them (their own words first), name one problem people like them hit, say what we offer in one sentence, and ask one easy yes or no. 45 to 75 words. The subject names their topic in 2 to 4 words.

1. The shape: premise, tension, bridge, ask

Four short paragraphs, 4 sentences, each under 25 words. It has to fit a phone screen.

1. PremiseLine 1 is about them, never about us. It never starts with "I" or "We" and never praises them ("sharp point", "spot on"). It builds on what they said instead.
2. TensionOne problem people like them run into, put as what's common, never as a claim about them. In our own words: no stock lead-ins like "The catch most teams hit".
3. BridgeWhat we offer, in one plain sentence tied to that problem. No feature list, no percentages or ROI claims, no buzzwords, no links.
4. AskOne yes or no question about interest, under 12 words, with no meeting or time in it. A yes leads to the call. It's the only question mark in the email.

Why problem before product: Gong's 28 million emails show pitching cuts replies by up to 57%. Why an interest ask: Gong's CTA study of 304,174 emails found asking about interest beats asking for a meeting on cold emails.

2. The premise ladder: what line 1 stands on

The writer always uses the highest step it has, in Becc Holland's order: content they wrote beats content they engaged with, which beats company facts. One premise per email; anything else waits for a follow-up.

3. Subject lines

  • 2 to 4 words, lowercase, naming their topic or priority in their own vocabulary: "bfcm email readiness", "true cac", "kuvola repeat purchase messaging".
  • A statement, never a question (Lavender: questions cut opens 56%). No first name (Salesloft via Lavender: 12% fewer replies). No numbers, no product name, no "quick question", no fake "re:" or "fwd:", never "your post".
  • Personalised to the topic, not the person: Belkins' 5.5 million emails show personalised subjects at 7% replies against 3% for generic.
  • Follow-ups reply in the same thread, so they keep the first subject.

4. Follow-ups

  • Each one reads like a short reply in the same thread (Instantly: about 30% better than a formal follow-up) and brings one new angle.
  • Follow-up 1: a different problem people like them hit. The middle ones: a different kind of value (time, revenue, or cost). The last: under 30 words, makes a no easy and leaves the door open.
  • Never repeats the first email's premise or bridge. No "just checking in", no "bumping this", at most one question.

5. What the code enforces

6. Before and after (Brevo campaign, names removed)

Before: all 33 drafts shared one subject and an offer-first body. After: 32 drafts (one rival removed) with 32 different subjects, 54 to 79 words, average 64.

A CRM director who wrote about BFCM email readiness

BEFORE · subject: email and sms, one billHi [first], Brevo runs email, SMS and WhatsApp for ecommerce stores in one platform, and we price by emails sent rather than by contacts. So a growing list doesn't inflate your bill. Most CRM leads I speak with are wrestling with channel costs that scale with list size, not with actual sending. If that resonates, worth a quick chat. Do you have 15 minutes this week?
AFTER · subject: bfcm email readinessHi [first], Nine days out is a tight window to fix authentication, list hygiene and send warming before BFCM hits. Many teams in that spot also find their email and SMS bills climb with list size, since pricing is usually per contact. We price by emails sent instead, and keep email and SMS on one platform. Worth comparing that model against your setup?

9. Sources

GitHub repos and agent skills (read in full)

  • coreyhaines31/marketingskills (cold-email and prospecting skills, 59k stars): the "delete the opener" test, interest asks, a two-step research process, and "no hook found" as a valid result.
  • ethanplusai/sellbird (4.8k stars): one signal per email, stale signals expire, and "a wrong specific is worse than a right generic".
  • growthenginenowoslawski/coldoutboundskills: posts from the last 3 months only, skip personal or charged posts, never invent a post.
  • Cold-IQ/ColdIQ-s-GTM-Skills: what the prospect wrote ranked as the most valuable data; strong vs lite hooks.
  • swan-gtm/gtm-skills (signal-anchored message): open on the change, never hint at how the lead was found.
  • oneshot-agent/oneshot-gtm: a 12 to 22 word opener with one verifiable fact, and no product in line 1.
  • naveedharri/benai-skills: cite a post only if it relates to what you sell; paraphrase, never quote.
  • julienamorgan/signal-prospecting-kit: use the topic, never the platform.
  • BrianRWagner/ai-marketing-claude-code-skills: personalisation tiers by how much is known.
  • tech-leads-club/agent-skills: a 3-line email (pain, proof, ask).
