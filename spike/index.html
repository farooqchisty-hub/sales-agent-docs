Sourcing spike: can free sources feed 40 contacts a day?

Milestone S of the sales agent plan, run 20 Oct 2026. Three sample businesses, free sources only (no Apollo, Hunter, ZeroBounce, ScrapeCreators, Apify or paid treg calls). Plain HTTP, DNS over HTTPS and free model tiers. No email was sent to anyone and no SMTP probing was done.

Overall verdict. Free sources solve company discovery for all three buyers, fast and in volume. They do not solve the contact layer: named decision makers with an email you can trust. Under the plan's own rule (send only to published addresses or a pattern the domain has proved) the three campaigns produced 7, 0 and 10 usable contacts from 140, 118 and 40 companies. That is 0.05, 0.00 and 0.25 usable contacts per company, so 40 a day would need roughly 800, unbounded, and 160 companies processed per day. Verdict: 40 a day per campaign is NOT reachable on free sources alone for any of the three. The bottleneck is email proof, not finding companies, and it is the same bottleneck for every industry, so it should be solved once in the shared layer of the framework.

Direction: a dynamic sourcing framework, not a fixed lead database

Per Farooq's direction, sourcing becomes a per-campaign plan generated from the buyer's own segment description, not a standard database. The spike shows which parts of that plan vary by segment (where companies come from) and which part is the same everywhere (finding the person and proving the email). The framework below keeps the variable part pluggable and puts the effort into the shared part.

  1. Segment brief in: the user describes the buyer in plain words (industry, geography, size, roles). The model turns it into a Source Plan: which source types to try, the exact queries or filters for each (tags, SIC or NAICS codes, registry names, search queries), the decision-maker roles, and fit rules.
  2. Source adapters are small plug-ins of a few generic types (table below). The planner fills their parameters; no adapter is written per industry. New adapters are only needed for new source types.
  3. Probe then commit: each planned source runs a small probe (about 20 companies) through the whole pipeline. Sources are ranked by usable contacts per company and per request, and the daily budget shifts to the winners. When a source runs dry or drops below threshold the planner proposes the next one.
  4. Shared contact layer, identical for every segment: resolve domain, crawl home, about, team, contact and sitemap pages, extract people and roles with a free model, add people from registries where the source has them (company officers, licence holders).
  5. Email proof ladder, also shared: published address, then a pattern proved by a published address at the same domain, then pattern evidence from the open web, then (optional valve) a verifier key. Guesses are stored but never sent without proof.
  6. Verification and gates as in the plan: syntax, MX over DNS over HTTPS, disposable and role lists, catch-all flag, fit score with reason, dedupe across campaigns.
  7. Yield ledger in source_jobs: per source and per step counts so the agent, and the buyer, can see where leads are lost and which fix (search key, verifier key, CSV) would unlock the most.

Method

  • Three sample businesses, each a full cold run (empty cache) on a laptop, all three running at once with 8 crawl workers each: US multi-location dental groups, ecommerce brands, UK mid-size logistics.
  • Discovery: OpenStreetMap Overpass, NPPES NPI registry, Tranco top 1M plus Shopify DNS check, Common Crawl CDX, Greenhouse and Lever board JSON, Companies House advanced search, filing history and officers pages, SEC EDGAR. Domain resolution by name guess plus homepage match, with DuckDuckGo and Brave HTML as fallback.
  • Crawl: plain fetch of home plus up to 5 linked about, team, contact or sitemap pages. Cloudflare email obfuscation and [at] style addresses decoded.
  • Extraction: one free model call per company (OpenRouter minimax-m3.1-flash free, with Workers AI as fallback, never needed). The same call judges fit against the seller and buyer description.
  • Email finding: published first, then a pattern proved by a published address that maps to a named person, then pattern from address shape, then a first.last guess (kept, never counted). Pattern hunt in the Common Crawl archive and a web search for "@domain".
  • Free verification: syntax, MX via cloudflare-dns.com, disposable list (disposable-email-domains), role list, security-gateway MX flagged as likely catch-all. Gravatar was also tried as an existence signal: 0 hits on 112 decision makers across 4 patterns each, so it was dropped. No SMTP, no sends.

Cheapest fixes

  • Optional verifier key on the guesses (first@ and first.last@ for each owner): the guesses already pass every free check at 86%, so a mailbox check is what turns them into sendable contacts. At 40 a day that is roughly 2,400 checks a month (2 patterns each).

  • Consider the role address as a first touch for this vertical: 12 fit groups had only an office address, and small groups often route it to the owner.

What this means for section 3B

  • Replace the fixed source list in 3B with a Source Plan generated per campaign from the segment brief, plus probe then commit across generic source adapters.
  • Keep company discovery free. It worked for every segment, within minutes, from a different source each time.
  • Make two optional buyer keys first class, both cheap and both shared across all segments: a web search API key (free tiers exist) and an email verifier key. The verifier is the single fix that moves all three campaigns, at roughly 1,200 to 2,400 checks a month for 40 contacts a day; pay-as-you-go verifiers price that in the low single-digit to low double-digit dollars a month (confirm current pricing). That sits next to the $5 running cost, so the bundle promise should say 'about $5 a month, plus an optional verifier for volume'.
  • Do not build on scraped search engines or the Common Crawl index for the daily cron. Both blocked or timed out in this spike.
  • Cloudflare Workers cannot open port 25, and SMTP probing was out of scope anyway, so free mailbox proof does not exist in the Worker. Bounce auto-pause stays as the backstop, not as a verifier.
  • Show the yield ledger in the launch gate preview: if a segment cannot produce 10 sourced leads, say which key or CSV would fix it before the user commits.

Limits of this spike

  • Samples are small (140, 118 and 40 companies) and one cold run each, so treat rates as direction, not forecasts.
  • Bounce numbers from milestone S were not measured: that needs real sends, which need Farooq's explicit permission. Pass rates here are free checks only, so a pass does not prove the mailbox exists.
  • Per hour figures are laptop wall time with all three runs sharing one connection and one free model key. The real limits in production are free model daily quotas and source rate limits, not CPU.
  • Hiring boards and Common Crawl host lists failed through index timeouts in the full run, so their yield is under-measured. LinkedIn and directory pages were not tested.
  • The free model judged fit generously in dental and ecom and strictly in logistics (13 of 40); fit rules would be tuned per campaign by the planner.
