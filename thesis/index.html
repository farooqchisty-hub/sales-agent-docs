0. The thesis

The agent loop (research, write, send, follow up, handle replies, book) is now commodity code, so charging seats for it is SaaS 1.0. We host the agent for free with tight monthly caps: it runs on a cheap model and the customer's own inbox, so a free user costs us about $0.60 a month. We charge for the fuel: volume past the caps, leads and signals (data), warmed inboxes (sending), premium writing, and outcomes (meetings, plus a human done-for-you tier). One prepaid wallet, priced per use, no seats.

Free has to deliver a real result on its own (replies from real prospects), or users churn before they ever hit a wall. The walls are placed where value is proven and cost starts: "I want more prospects", "find me more leads", "send from more inboxes", "write it better".

Hosting everything means we own the relationship: every signup, their ideal customer, their usage and their results live in our database. Users who hit the caps are qualified upgrade leads we can see, and Closefast itself can run our own outreach to them.

1. What the market is telling us

Greg Isenberg, "SaaS is dead?"

  • The tool is now easy to build, so give it away.
  • Free users pay in data, which builds network effects and a moat.
  • Upsell intelligence (agents, billed per token or per outcome) and human service (weekly or monthly fee).
  • SMBs trend to free; enterprises still pay for security and reassurance.

Explee (ex AutoGTM)

  • Hosted, no subscription. Lead finding free, about $0.03 to $0.04 per email sent, $30 trial credit (card required).
  • Sends from its own pool of pre-warmed inboxes; replies stay in Explee.
  • Claims 76,371 hot leads for 7,171 companies; median $9 per hot lead.
  • Weak spots in reviews: no own domain or sender names, sends before leads are approved, odd replies.

Jungler

  • $99 to $299 a month for data only: LinkedIn engagers, enriched, sent to other tools.
  • Bills every raw like or comment, even people your filter drops.
  • Never sends anything.

Closefast today

  • Approval-first agent with reply handling, booking, guardrails and duplicate-send protection, live on the Brevo pilot.
  • Every table already carries a workspace id, so going multi-tenant is an extension, not a rewrite.
  • Today's offer ($49 license, $99 setup, self-install) gets replaced by this model.

5. Inboxes: how Explee does it, how we would

Explee: a shared pool

  • Sends from Explee-owned, pre-warmed inboxes. Each prospect gets one inbox for the whole thread.
  • Replies land inside Explee; the agent answers and books.
  • Paid per email sent (about $0.03 to $0.04). No setup at all, which is the selling point.
  • The cost: customers can't send from their own brand, domain or local sender names.

Us: branded inboxes, one click

  • Customer picks a lookalike domain (acme.com becomes getacme.com). We register it on our Cloudflare account at cost, so we can switch inbox vendors any time.
  • InboxKit API creates Google or Microsoft inboxes (3 per domain), sets SPF, DKIM and DMARC, and runs warmup. One InboxKit workspace per customer.
  • Closefast pulls the logins into the inbox drivers it already has (SMTP/IMAP, Gmail, Microsoft) and keeps sending caps per inbox.
  • Customers already on Instantly: we place an Instantly order with their key instead (no margin, no ops).

Watch-outs. Most cheap Google inboxes seem to ride regional reseller pricing, so a Google policy change could suspend a batch at once: spread across two vendors. Fresh inboxes need about 2 weeks of warmup, so "send today" means pre-warmed at roughly double. Before building, confirm which login fields InboxKit's credentials endpoint returns.

Why not copy Explee's shared pool? It's the fastest start, but every customer shares one reputation and we become the sender of record for their claims. A small shared pool could still power a free "first 50 emails" trial later, behind our own content gates.

6. Where the Jungler module fits

  • Free hook: "See who engaged with your LinkedIn posts" is the free in-app signal and a standalone free tool page for top-of-funnel; reaching those people is the next click.
  • Paid: competitor pages, creators and keyword searches, metered per matched person.
  • Why ours wins: Jungler stops at a list for $99 a month and up. Ours goes signal to booked meeting, and bills only for people who match.

7. Economics

Free user

  • About $0.60 a month at every cap; less on average.
  • 1,000 active free users: about $600 a month.
  • Covered when about 1.5% of free users spend $60 a month (at 68% gross margin).

Paid user: 500 new prospects a month (2,000 emails), our leads, 4 inboxes on 2 branded domains

  • They pay: 400 extra prospects $12 + leads $40 + inboxes $20 + domains $2.50 = $74.50 a month. Explee for the same 2,000 emails: $70 to $80, from Explee's domains.
  • We spend: about $23.50. Gross margin about 68%.
  • Add 200 signal matches: +$30 for about $2 of data.

Data, signals and extra prospects carry the margin (5x to 30x cost). Inboxes and premium writing run thin on purpose: they're the on-ramp and the quality lever.

8. What hosting adds to the build

  • Multi-tenant: signup, login, workspaces, per-tenant quotas and a usage ledger (the tables already carry a workspace id).
  • Inbox connect: Google requires app verification plus an annual third-party security assessment (CASA) for apps that read Gmail, and Microsoft asks for publisher verification. Start this first; it gates the one-click Gmail connect. SMTP/IMAP works meanwhile.
  • Billing: Stripe wallet, top-ups, auto top-up, usage shown live.
  • Abuse controls for free: work-email signup only, one free account per domain, the sending inbox must match that domain, the first 10 emails need approval, daily caps, the content gates we already run, auto-pause on bounces or spam complaints.
  • Model routing: Haiku 5.5 by default, Sonnet 5.5 for premium; prompt caching on the shared brief to cut repeat input cost.
