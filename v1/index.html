0. Blockers, ranked

#BlockerWhy it blocksWay through
1Entity and accountsClosefast runs today on the Cloudflare account "Sheik.farooq@pushowl.com's Account" and the pushowl treg team. A SaaS that takes money needs its own legal entity, Cloudflare, Stripe, treg or provider accounts, GitHub org and domains, and a clear contract for the Brevo pilot (who owns the code, data and customers).Decide the operating entity; open separate accounts; keep the Brevo pilot on the current setup until then.
2Data licensingShowing provider data to our customers and keeping a shared person index may breach a provider's terms. LinkedIn-derived data carries legal risk (Proxycurl shut down in 2025 after LinkedIn sued).Check each provider's resale and display terms (Fetchin, BlitzAPI, Bright Data, TikHub, AnyAPI) before wiring them in; keep providers swappable; keep an erasure list.
3We become the senderFree sends from our pool, so complaints, blocklists and legal notices land on us.Terms of service, acceptable use policy, abuse inbox, country rules, B2C block, content gates, auto-pause (sections 4 and 5). Legal review before launch.
4Pool warmup lead timeNew inboxes need about 2 weeks or more of warmup before cold sends; the pool must be warm on launch day.Order and warm the first pool inboxes early in the build, or buy pre-warmed at roughly double.
5InboxKit credentialsWhether its API returns SMTP/IMAP logins or only OAuth decides the whole send and receive path.Buy 1 inbox and test send, receive and forwarding before building the pool manager.
6Google policy riskCheap Google inboxes likely ride regional reseller pricing; a policy change could suspend a batch.Two vendors in the pool (InboxKit plus Primeforge or Maildoso), never more than half the pool with one.
7Prospect discovery at scaleThe pilot's free web search is rate-limited (it failed on 28 Oct).Paid search and data providers behind the source router, budgeted per tenant.
8Stripe and taxNeeds the entity; prepaid credits need tax handling by country.Stripe plus Stripe Tax once the entity exists.
9Gmail app verificationOnly for "connect your own Gmail" (paid, later). Not a V1 blocker.Start the verification when V1 is stable.

1. V1 scope

In V1

  • Signup, login, one company per account, free team seats
  • Onboarding from a website: ICP, sources, B2B or B2C check
  • Campaigns with a goal (conversation, meeting, page visit, custom)
  • Prospects from database, local and CSV; exclusion lists
  • LinkedIn signals, opt-in (competitor page or keyword)
  • Writing with gates, approval queue, autopilot settings
  • Sending from our warm pool; reply handling; booking
  • Free starter boost and always-free allowance; prepaid wallet
  • Dashboard, lead alerts, weekly digest
  • Our admin console, kill switches, invariants, cost tracking

After V1

  • Branded domains and inboxes (paid), connect own Gmail or Outlook
  • Tracked links and conversion webhooks
  • Slack, CRM sync (HubSpot, Brevo), API and MCP
  • Agency mode (client workspaces), white label
  • LinkedIn as a sending channel (account-ban risk)
  • Done-for-you service, enterprise

3. Fix before building on top

  • Reply-poll starvation: the fix (seen set = every stored reply, own copies dropped before the 10-slot cut) exists only on the WS branch as ab4d081. Port it to main. The Brevo instance already shows the pattern (listed 3, stored 0).
  • Booking detection is link-only; bookings are marked by hand today.
  • "Ran out of sources" fires for list-only campaigns.
  • Web search rate limit breaks sourcing (blocker 7).
  • Audit the creator engine's 30 silent defects against Closefast: NULL subjects, retry ceilings, a pending cold row silencing an owed reply, threading by a list of kinds, new lanes not inheriting guards, "sent" vs delivered, signature lines read as names.

4. Scenarios and edge cases

A. Signup and trust

CaseRule
Signup with Gmail, Yahoo or a disposable domainBlocked; work email only.
Work email domain differs from the website domainProve ownership: a code sent to an address at the website's domain, or a DNS TXT record. Stops people posing as brands they don't own.
Second free account on the same company domainJoins the existing account as a teammate; no second allowance.
Prohibited business (gambling, adult, weapons, crypto schemes, drugs, debt collection, lead resale)Classifier on the website and brief; blocked at onboarding with a plain reason.
Bot signupsCloudflare Turnstile on signup; rate limit per IP.
Brand-new accountTrust ramp: first 10 emails need approval, 10 sends on day one, full free cap after the first clean week.

B. Onboarding from a website

CaseRule
Site down, JavaScript-only or parkedFall back to 3 questions: what you sell, who buys it, where.
Several products or buyer typesAgent proposes up to 3 ICPs; each campaign picks one.
Buyers are consumers (homebuyers, patients, shoppers)No cold email to individuals. Offer B2B angles (partners, suppliers, local businesses) or explain why cold email doesn't fit.
Buyers are on LinkedIn (SaaS, agencies, B2B services)Offer the opt-in signal card; otherwise never mention signals.
Local business buyers (restaurants, clinics, shops)Local source (maps, directories); role inboxes allowed.
Non-English site or marketWrite in the prospect's language from a supported list; otherwise ask.
Agency selling for clientsV1: one company per account. Agency mode later.

C. Campaign setup

CaseRule
Sender identityReal name, title, company and postal address required before anything sends (footer). No address, no send.
Booking link dead or not a known booking toolValidate on save; warn; block a meeting goal without a working link.
Page goal URLMust load and sit on the business's own domain (no redirects to third-party sites, which also stops phishing).
Facts the agent may claimA facts list built from the website and confirmed by the user; anything else (numbers, client names, results) is blocked by the claims gate.
User never approves the first 10Nothing sends; nudges at 24 and 72 hours.

D. Prospects and sources

CaseRule
Same person from two sources or campaignsDedupe on email, LinkedIn URL, then name plus company; merge the reasons.
Existing customers, partners, competitors, own staffExclusion lists (CSV, domains, later CRM). Checked at queue time and again at send time.
Many people at one companyAt most 2 in an active sequence at once; a negative reply or "nobody here" stops the whole company.
Catch-all or risky emailNever from the free pool (protects everyone's reputation); allowed with a cap on paid branded inboxes.
Role inbox (info@, hello@)Only when no named person exists, mostly local businesses; lower priority.
Email older than 90 daysRe-verify before sending.
Imported CSV bounces badlyVerify on import (small charge); pause the list if bounces pass 5%.
Two tenants target the same person from the poolOne cold sequence per person per 60 days across all pool sends; the second tenant's prospect waits or is skipped.
Person opted out or complained via any tenant on the poolGlobal pool suppression. "Not interested" stays tenant-only.
EU or UK sole traders, CanadaCountry rules table (section 5); V1 sends only to the allowed list.

E. Signals

CaseRule
Competitor page with huge engagementCap per run (latest 5 posts, first 500 engagers each); estimate monthly matches and cost before the user saves it.
Engagers who aren't buyersHeadline triage drops the competitor's own staff, the user's staff, job seekers, students, vendors and recruiters.
Same person on several signalsOne prospect, all reasons kept.
Already in a sequence or excludedSkipped before enrichment (no cost).
Stale engagementOnly the last 30 days; first touch no sooner than 24 hours after it.
CopyNever mention the engagement or name the competitor; the signal only picks the angle.
Provider down or bannedFallback provider; the signal pauses with a notice instead of failing silently.

F. Writing and content

CaseRule
Made-up claimsClaims gate against the facts list (exists, extend to numbers and client names).
Wrong company or person name from scrapingName and company must match the prospect record; salutation check (exists); a failed research step falls back to a plain, correct opener.
DeliverabilityPlain text, no images or attachments, no link in the opener by default, at most 1 link later, no URL shorteners, no open-tracking pixels from the pool, spam-word check.
ThreadingFollow-ups thread on the opener by rule (not a list of kinds); subject never empty.
Instructions hidden in a scraped page or a replyTreated as text, never as instructions; the writer has no tools; gates run after every draft.
Model refuses or returns emptyRetry with a ceiling, then the fallback model, then hold for review. Never skip a planned touch silently.

G. Sending and the pool

CaseRule
Which inbox sendsEach tenant pinned to 1 or 2 pool inboxes; a prospect's whole thread stays on one inbox.
Inbox burned mid-threadStops new sends; keeps receiving until its open threads close; then retired.
BounceHard bounce suppresses the address and refunds the credit; inbox pauses above 3% over its last 100 sends; tenant pauses above 5%.
Spam complaintGlobal suppression; tenant review; pause above 0.1% over 1,000 sends.
Holidays and weekendsSkipped per the prospect's country.
UnsubscribeFooter line plus a List-Unsubscribe header (mailto and one-click), honoured instantly.

CaseRule

Prospect sends their own booking linkFlag to the user as a lead needing action.
Page goal clicksTracked links on a separate tracking domain (never a sending domain), with UTM tags.

CaseRule
Allowance runs out mid-sequenceA prospect is charged once when started, so its follow-ups always finish; replies are always handled free.
Wallet emptyNew prospects stop; in-flight sequences and replies continue.
Spend safetyReserve credits before spending, commit after; the balance can never go negative; every usage event is idempotent.
Price surpriseShow the expected cost before saving a signal or a campaign.
Allowance timingResets on the signup anniversary; never rolls over.

J. Data rights and security

CaseRule
A prospect asks to be erasedGlobal erasure list: removed from the shared index and every tenant, never re-imported.
"How did you get my email?"Fixed, honest answer (public business information) plus opt-out.
A query missing its tenant filterAll SQL through one tenant-scoped helper; a test fails the build on any raw query without workspace_id.
Stored secrets (booking links, webhooks, inbox logins)Encrypted (the MASTER_KEY scheme exists); never shown back in full.
Inactive tenantsProspect data deleted after a set retention period; export on request.
Staff accessAdmin console behind Cloudflare Access; impersonation logged.

8. Invariants and monitoring (from 40 paid-for traps)

  • Every stage reports listed, processed and stored; alert when listed stays above zero and stored stays at zero.
  • Oldest due item per queue; alert when work waits too long (starvation).
  • Every queued command type has a consumer; dead-letter queues are watched.
  • "Sent" means delivered by the provider, not handed over.
  • One shared guard function for every lane (opt-out, excluded, suppressed, paused), so a new lane can't skip one.
  • Each invariant runs on its own, so one broken check can't silence the rest.
  • Cost per tenant and per provider per day; alert on spikes.
  • A canary tenant sends daily to seed inboxes at Gmail and Outlook to measure inbox placement.
  • Eval sets: 200 real replies for classification and 50 openers for facts and tone; a prompt change can't ship if they regress.

10. Build order

  1. Foundations: entity and accounts decision, tenant scoping, auth, admin console skeleton, invariants framework, port the reply-poll fix.
  2. Pool sending: InboxKit test, pool manager, per-inbox Durable Object, push reply ingestion, footer and unsubscribe, health and pauses, global suppression. Start warming inboxes here.
  3. Onboarding and sources: website to ICP, B2B or B2C check, source router (database, local, CSV), verification, dedupe, exclusions, country rules.
  4. Writing and replies: model router, facts list, expanded gates, reply outcomes, approval queue, goals and booking.
  5. Free plan and wallet: allowances, trust ramp, Stripe wallet, metering ledger, refunds, abuse controls.
  6. Signals (opt-in) and digests.
  7. Hardening: eval sets, canary tenant, staging soak, legal pages.

Then the ads.brevo.com and Brevo ecommerce pilots run as tenants on V1.
