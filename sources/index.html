Sources, pipeline, outreach: buy prospects from any source, then choose who gets emailed and when

Farooq's review on 7 Nov 2026 found that the core flow was missing. You can't search for people, see a count and buy them, the way Explee does. Signals is one source among several, not the product. This plan makes buying prospects the core loop, adds sources to it as plug-ins (people search, signals, your list, web research, later Reddit or any connector), and separates being in the pipeline (bought, yours) from being in outreach (the agent is emailing them, starting when you say). It follows the Signals plan and the Credits plan.

In one line: Find prospects (pick a source, see the count, sample and price, buy N) → they land in the Pipeline, tagged and charged → you select who to start, when, and how much the agent may send without you → the agent runs outreach. Bring your own list and inboxes and the agent is free.

How others do it

Explee (the reference flow)

  1. No signup to start: paste a website (or answer three questions). The agent proposes customer segments with fit scores, finds companies, then people, then writes the emails, and shows sample contacts with "and N more". (home, demo)
  2. Paywall at the end: email and card, permission to email on your behalf, $30 trial credit.
  3. Per campaign: setup, lead search, review (an Approve button), sending. With autopilot on, the list auto-approves after a 12-hour review window. You approve the whole list; picking single leads isn't shown in their API. Some users report sends started sooner than they expected. (API spec, Trustpilot)
  4. Billing: finding leads is free. They charge $0.04 per email sent from their own warmed mailboxes; leads without a verified email are never billed; replies are free. Data credits (1 credit = $0.01) apply on the API: 1 per person searched, 1.5 or 5 per email found. (pricing)
  5. Sources: their own database (105M companies, 536M people), Google Maps places, CSV upload (up to 30,000). No intent signals.
What we copy from Explee: results before payment (prospects and drafted emails from just a website), and a whole flow that ends in "emails ready to send".
Where we differ: we charge per verified prospect plus per email from our inboxes, not per email alone; you pick exactly who is emailed and when; signals are a source; and with your own list and inboxes, the agent is free.

The new flow

FIND PROSPECTS PIPELINE OUTREACH (pick a source) (bought or imported: yours) (the agent emails them) People search --+ Signals --+--> qualify --> charge --> [ Not started ] --select--> [ Scheduled ] --> [ In outreach ] Your list --+ (gates, (only a tags: source, role, or a rule start date cadence, replies, Web research --+ tags) verified country, size, heat, picks them you chose booking Later: Reddit, --+ email) fit, list name | any connector Replied / Booked / Lost / Opted out
  1. Find prospects. One hub, a card per source. Each card shows its price per prospect and its last yield.
  2. See before you pay. Every source answers an estimate first: how many match, 10 sample rows (name and email hidden until bought), price, and how many your credits cover.
  3. Buy N. A slider or number. Only people with a verified email who pass the gates are charged. Duplicates of people already in your pipeline are free and skipped.
  4. Pipeline. Everyone bought or imported lands here as "Not started", tagged and grouped by the list they came from.
  5. Start outreach. Select rows (or a saved filter), pick a campaign, pick when (now, a date, or spread over N days), and pick how much the agent may send without you.

One contract for every source, so new ones plug in without touching the pipeline:

  • estimate(spec) returns the count, 10 masked samples, cost per prospect and expected yield.
  • fetch(spec, n, cursor) returns raw candidates, then the next cursor.
  • Everything after that is shared: dedupe (suppression list, already in pipeline, opted out anywhere), the "can't buy" gates, tags, email find and verify, charge, then addToPipeline().

A saved search is a list. "Get 100 more" continues its cursor, so no row is bought twice.

Pipeline (the Prospects tab, renamed)

  • Rows: everyone bought or imported. Columns: name, title, company, source and list, tags (role, country, size, platform, heat, fit), outreach state, bought date.
  • Outreach state: Not started → Scheduled → In outreach → Replied / Interested / Booked / Won / Lost, plus Paused, Opted out and Bounced.
  • Filters and saved views: source, list, campaign, any tag, state, heat, fit, date. For example, "Hot signals, founders, US" is one click.
  • Bulk actions: Start outreach (campaign, when, approval), Pause, Move to another campaign (only before outreach starts), Export CSV, Wrong match (teaches the gates).
  • Row drawer: why they're here (the signal quote and post, or the search filters they matched), the company, and the history.
  • Signals freshness: heat cools with time, and Home nudges ("9 hot signal prospects from this week aren't started yet"). There's no forced wait. Copy never mentions the post, so emailing soon after someone engaged doesn't read as creepy.

Onboarding

  1. Website → we build the brief: what you sell, who buys, proof (built).
  2. "Where should prospects come from?" Cards: Search people (recommended) · LinkedIn signals · Upload your list (the agent is free) · Web research for niches. Pick one or more.
  3. Set up each source with a live estimate. People search arrives with filters prefilled from your website and a count. Signals proposes its watches (built). Your list is an upload.
  4. Buy the first batch with welcome credits. 1,500 credits buy about 187 people-search prospects or 37 signal prospects.
  5. Connect an inbox (your own keeps the agent free) or use our warm inboxes.
  6. Pipeline → select → Start outreach. Test mode previews come to you first.

Economics: can we charge only for qualified prospects?

Yes, as long as the data cost per qualified prospect stays at or under 30% of its price (a 70% margin).

SourceData cost per qualified prospectPriceMargin
People search (BlitzAPI)$0.005 to $0.02$0.0875 to 94%
Signals (pilot, measured)$0.07 to $0.13$0.4069 to 82%
Web research (current engine)about $0.03 to $0.08 (estimate, no hosted volume yet)$0.08Thin. Keep it for niches, and measure before scaling.

People-search maths: about 2.4 rows per email found (41% hit) × $0.0005, plus $0.0005 for the hit and about $0.003 to verify. That's roughly $0.005, or about $0.01 to $0.02 when the gates reject half the rows.

Explee shows the other way to price: data looks free and the cost is folded into $0.04 per email sent. A prospect who gets three emails pays $0.12. That works because their data is their own database. Ours costs about $0.005 to $0.02 a prospect through BlitzAPI, so either model clears 70%. We keep per-prospect pricing, because the customer owns what they bought whether or not they email it, and sending is billed only on our inboxes.

Why others bill raw rows: it moves the yield risk (a narrow ideal customer, missing emails) onto the customer, it's easy to audit, and "qualified" invites disputes. We take that risk on, with four controls:

  1. An estimate before every buy.
  2. Gates that are mechanical, not taste: a verified email, not a rival, a real company.
  3. The yield guard and the daily data cap for free accounts.
  4. A per-order spend ceiling.

Free plan: your leads, your inboxes

  • Bring your own list and connect your own inboxes, and the whole agent is free: drafting, follow-ups, replies, booking, summaries, autopilot. No model key needed.
  • Fair use is 1,500 new prospects contacted a month. Past that, agent work is 3 credits per prospect.
  • Free workspaces (no purchase yet) run on the cheapest open models: Workers AI by default, with Llama 3.3 70B on OpenRouter as the only fallback. Our LLM cost is a fraction of a cent per prospect.
  • These still cost credits: data we find (people search, signals, web research), finding missing emails for your list, our warm inboxes, premium research and writing.
  • Status: being built (7 Nov). It ships with this plan's first deploy.
